## Primary research and conclusions

1. **Wu & Aguayo / McMaster University, Digital Marketing Fundamentals (2025/2026), original PDF chapter 2, Sections 1–12:** buyer priority, purpose of site, understandable first screen, mobile navigation, relevant evidence, a meaningful conversion/action, message over decoration, A/B tests for measured impact. An attractive layout alone is not proof of an effective business site. Current source: `money-and-business/complete-open-university-books-2026/sales-and-marketing/2026-digital-marketing-fundamentals-mcmaster-university-2026-385a258fa.pdf` on Raspi.
2. **MIT Sloan 15.810, original 2015 Marketing Management lecture 1 and Strategic Positioning / Voice of Customer notes:** customer need, company capabilities, competing solutions, collaborators and context come before tactics. Customer needs must be stated from a buyer's perspective rather than a programmer's feature vocabulary. Verified original archive `money-and-business/mit-open-courseware/2015-15-810-marketing-management-analytics-frameworks-and-applications-fall-2015.zip`.
3. **Fossitt et al., Introduction to Market Research (2024), modules 2, 4.1 and 5.1–5.4:** define and validate the actual commercial decision problem and primary audience. Secondary research cannot confirm specific buyers' willingness to pay; a plausible developer scenario is still a hypothesis. Original university-hosted full text in the Raspi source catalog.
4. **Strategic Marketing Tactics (2025), chapters 4 and 7:** segmentation, a distinct target market, specific application, credible differentiation and customer value. This does not prove uniqueness of SWAAG.
5. **Vesterinen et al. (2026), The Impact of Generative AI on B2B Marketing Processes: Evidence from Industrial Firms:** 43 exploratory interviews across 18 industrial firms and 10 vendors describe barriers to process integration and structured human control. Not a representative SWAAG buyer survey, but relevant motivation for source-evidenced tool/workflow integration.
6. **The Missing Big Idea (Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 2026), 548 historical Super Bowl advertisements and YouGov data:** distinctive defensible messages can shape some tracked funnel outcomes; observed consumer-ad effects are not a B2B software conversion estimate. The study does *not* justify making an unverified unique-selling-point claim.
7. **Born, Building Trust in Startup Communication (Springer, 2024), Nikola single-case study:** use technical evidence with truthful boundaries; avoid vague futuristic claims, synthetic demos disguised as successful deployments, fabricated testimonials and misleading prospective-client effects.
8. **Cheng, Li & Pavlou, Information Transparency, Choice Confidence and Customer Retention (Management Science, 2026):** consumer insurance field/lab findings support precision about scope and evaluative evidence, with no direct proven impact on software-site conversion.
9. **Google Search Central, SEO Starter Guide and Helpful, Reliable, People-First Content:** clear pages serving people first, truthful primary evidence and discoverable titles. This does not imply guaranteed traffic or Google rankings.

The complete first-party originals and edition/reprint identities are retained in Raspi's private knowledge library and its catalog; no copyrighted book material or internal business file was uploaded to this website.

## Audience and offer

**Priority audience hypothesis:** developers and technical teams with local/self-hosted models who need permitted tool execution, longer task workflows and inspectable progress. Relevant visitor question: *Our local model can answer questions, but what must we build to let it safely manage multi-step computer tasks using our tools?*

**SWAAG's actual role:** a local-first, evolving open-source agent runtime with a persistent user orchestrator, durable workers/events and configurable tool access. It offers code for developers to inspect and potentially build on; success with any specific task depends on connected model, tools, permissions, requirements and tests. The public GitHub README, manual, design principles, code and tests are credible implementation proof, **not** guarantees of finished deployments, error-free autonomy or hours saved.

**Critical rights distinction:** SWAAG's public repository is licensed MIT. The publicly distributed code is already available free, including commercial reuse under the license. Do not advertise selling exclusive rights to that public code. Separately developed deliverables or additional technology arrangements may be discussed only with actual verified rights and agreement.

**Separate buyer segments:** Late Order Monitor is for Wix store operators reviewing already-paid unfulfilled orders; KnowMoreDiRT is research toward source-backed answers to existing files, not a verified enterprise product; industrial 3D models serve visualization customers and have separate listings. They must not compete for equal first-screen attention on the software buyer's page.

## Marketing layout contract

The homepage leads with a specific buyer problem and flagship SWAAG use case. It separates actual implemented capabilities, intended benefits, illustrative scenarios, readiness and GitHub code proof. Published Wix software and KMD research remain explicit but secondary. Other public projects are one optional source-code evidence section and a fuller secondary portfolio, not nine interchangeable sales products.

The next action must be clear: developer can inspect free SWAAG code immediately, while a commercial decision-maker can discuss a **separately scoped** problem, relevant technology or rights. Bounded agreements, no universal support entitlement and no implied employment are important owner constraints.

## Remaining commercial evidence gap

Buyer need, price, conversion and differentiation are **not validated**. The next useful marketing experiment would collect actual permissioned interviews or anonymous first-screen comprehension checks from relevant technology decision-makers, determine what alternatives they already use, and test qualified inquiries rather than page views. No actual visitors were interviewed and no tracking cookies or third-party analytics are deployed by this research.
